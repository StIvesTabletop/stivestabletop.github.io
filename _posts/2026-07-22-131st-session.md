Lots of exploring distant lands this session, from Ireland to Arabia and Lost islands to Middle Earth.

The first short game was new to club [Money][Mon] another game designed by the prolific Reiner Kniza. This is a trading up game, players all start with 6 randomly dealt denominations of money from 7 currencies and two sets of 4 money cards are dealt to the table. Then in a secret simultaneous bid players offer some of their money cards to swap. Starting with the highest bidder players swap their cards for another set either of the two on the table or sets offered by the other players. Once they are done their cards are safe and play continues til everyone has done a swap. The aim is to collect sets of the same currency totalling more than 100 or 200 and triplets of the same currency and value. When the money draw pile is exhausted everyone counts up their sets. With it being a new game a couple of players took a few rounds to work out a swapping strategy. In the end one player had managed some impressive sets and most of the bonus silver and gold cards and was far ahead of the rest. A fun game that will hopefully be back to try again and test some more strategies.

Three players tried [Inis][I], last played in [session 35][35]. Inis means Island but also stories in Irish according to Diarmuid the games owner and teacher for the night. It is an area control game with three possible victory conditions relating to having clans present in 6 territories, being in territories with 6 or more total sanctuaries or being a chieftain over 6 opposing clans. Clans can occupy territories peacefully if both sides agree or take over a territory through a clash. At the start of each round 4 action cards each are drafted by the players through a passing on method similar to 7 Wonders. These cards give varying actions including exploring or placing new territories, moving clans, adding to clans, acquiring epic cards, blocking other players moves, building sanctuaries or citadels or having a clash. Our game was fairly peaceful, only a few clashes and some diplomacy to share territories. A few epic tales were told like Dagda's harp and Bran's voyage. But Dairmuid's experience led to a decisive victory after an attempt by Kathy to help Reynaldo attack Diarmuid's citadel by drawing some troops into a neighbouring territory backfired by giving him control of more areas. 


[Lord of the Rings: Fate of the Fellowship][LOTRFoF] was back for a second play (since [session 124][124]). This time the fellowship was made up of Gandalf and Gollum (led by James), Legolas, Frodo & Sam (led by Richard), Arwen and Gimli (led by Neil), and Aragorn and Boromir (led by Jeremy). They had four quests to complete apart from throwing the one ring into Mount Doom (... Mr Frodo). 

The game started with the fall of Helms Deep, and Frodo being spotted legging it out of the Shire, so Hope was at a low ebb for quite a well during the beginning of the game and it looked like the fellowship might lose. But though Saruman had a quick win taking Helms Deep, he had left Isengard undefended so Gandalf and Arwen walked in and turned it into a Haven to stop any more foul play. Gandalf then had to rush off to Confront the Balrog in the Mines of Moria, so was lost to us for a while (he came back after a couple of rounds, more powerful than before!).

To take back Helms Deep; Boromir brought many troops from Gondor and Rohan, but even with Tom Bombadil's help his armies were decimated by Nazgul. So it fell to Arwen and Aragorn to bring Elves (and with a little Orc infighting) finally helped Boromir to retake Helms Deep and to break Saruman's staff!

Meanwhile it looked like Minas Tirith would fall as Sauron used the distraction of Helms Deep to send his orcs out, luckily with the help of Lambas bread, Legolas & Gimli rushed to Gondor to unseat Denethor and rally the troops to protect the city! Shortly after, Arwen and Aragorn arrived and unfurlled the King's banner. Hope was at an all time high!

Unfortunately Frodo and Sam had been left in the Misty Mountains and all the Nazgul had found him there and the eye of Sauron was focussed on him too, and he had to evade multiple searches which sent Hope dashing back down to nearly zero. They had to use up some precious rings to avoid Hope getting to zero.

With all the quests but the One Ring complete, the fellowship then concentrated on helping Frodo obtain more rings and to finally meet up with Gollum and sneak into Mordor. Unfortunately time was running short (the player deck was nearly exhausted), and so were the rings (we needed 5 to complete the final quest), when disaster struck, Sauron in a cunning switch of troop movements sent his Orcs not to Minas Tirith, but to the Woodland realm, where there were no defenders (or fellowhship as they were mostly in Gondor)! Hope was lost and so was the game as the realm fell to the dark one.

A new roll and write game for club [My City][MyC], also by Reiner Kniza, to finish the evening. The 3 gamers tried the first 3 scenario's which gradually introduce the concepts. Dice rolls set which shape (and type of) buildings need to be fitted onto the player grids, around the river and trying to avoid trees but cover boulders. The second scenario introduces building types and trying to group the same type together for bonus scores. While the third scenario introduces a well with bonus points for placing 4 separate buildings around it. A simple concept for the end of the evening with all players having the same opportunities. Whilst there were a few swings in scores between rounds the totals across the 3 rounds were quite close with only 3 points separating first and last places.
